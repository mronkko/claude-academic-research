# Catherine Welch — Trinity College Dublin, Ireland

Catherine Welch is a senior qualitative methodologist in international business and management, now at Trinity College Dublin (previously University of Sydney and UNSW Sydney). Her work is foundational to debates on case study methodology in IB and management: she is best known for the JIBS Decade Award-winning "Theorising from case studies" (2010) and its retrospective on contextualized explanation, plus influential critiques of qualitative-research evaluation criteria, the Gioia template, and cross-language interviewing/translation. She is squarely a qualitative / methods-development scholar — quantitative submissions are out of scope for her. (Note: her OpenAlex record A5012939820 has merged-in namesakes — neutrino physics, fertility/PGT, and educational-assessment authors — so works_count and recent-works lists are inflated; the IB/qualitative-methods works are the genuine record.)

**Representative method papers:**
- Welch, Piekkari, Plakoyiannaki & Paavilainen-Mäntymäki (2011), "Theorising from case studies: Towards a pluralist future for international business research," *Journal of International Business Studies* — typology of four case-based theorizing approaches; the field-defining contribution.
- Welch, Paavilainen-Mäntymäki, Piekkari & Plakoyiannaki (2022), "Reconciling theory and context: How the case study can set a new agenda for IB research," *JIBS* — Decade Award retrospective advancing contextualized explanation.
- Welch & Piekkari (2017), "How should we (not) judge the 'quality' of qualitative research? A re-assessment of current evaluative criteria in International Business," *Journal of World Business* — critique of evaluative criteria for qualitative work.
- Mees-Buss, Welch & Piekkari (2020), "From Templates to Heuristics: How and Why to Move Beyond the Gioia Methodology," *Organizational Research Methods* — argues against template-driven coding, proposing a hermeneutic alternative.
- Piekkari, Welch & Paavilainen (2008), "The Case Study as Disciplinary Convention," *Organizational Research Methods* — systematic review of case-study practice in IB journals.
