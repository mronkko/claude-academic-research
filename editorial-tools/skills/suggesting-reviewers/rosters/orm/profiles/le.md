# Jane K. Lê — WHU – Otto Beisheim School of Management, Germany

Jane K. Lê (also published as Jane Lê / J. K. Lê) is a senior organization and strategy scholar at WHU – Otto Beisheim School of Management, working in the strategy-as-practice tradition with a deep methodological commitment to qualitative inquiry. Her substantive work spans paradox and tension management, coordinating, materiality and sociomateriality in strategy, and frontline strategy work, almost always through longitudinal, ethnographic, and process designs. Crucially for ORM, she also writes explicitly about *how* qualitative research is done — methodological innovation, ethnographic textwork, qualitative restudy designs, and analytic approaches to theorizing from qualitative data — which makes her a methodologist as much as a substantive researcher. Her orientation is best classified as mixed-or-methodologist: the underlying empirics are qualitative, but a large share of her output is method-development scholarship that would referee well on either qualitative or general methods grounds. With ~77 works, an h-index of 16, and high-FWCI papers in *Organization Science*, *Organization Studies*, and *Strategic Organization*, she is a clearly senior figure; she is a sitting ORM Associate Editor and so is not eligible as a reviewer.

**Representative method papers:**
- Lê & Schmid (2020), "The Practice of Innovating Research Methods," *Organizational Research Methods* — develops a two-layered "iceberg" model of how scholars innovate across data generation, analysis, and presentation; her flagship first-party ORM methods contribution.
- Jarzabkowski, Bednarek & Lê (2014), "Producing Persuasive Findings: Demystifying Ethnographic Textwork in Strategy and Organization Research," *Strategic Organization* — explicit guidance on crafting and writing ethnographic findings.
- Köhler, Smith, Greckhamer & Lê (2025), "Feature Topic for ORM: Advanced Analytic Approaches to Theorize From Qualitative Research," *Organizational Research Methods* — frames advanced analytic strategies for theorizing from qualitative data.
- Welch, Köhler, Rumyantseva, Wright et al. (2025), "How to Conduct a Qualitative Restudy: Research Designs to Reevaluate Existing Theories," *AOM Proceedings* — restudy designs for systematically reinvestigating prior qualitative findings.
