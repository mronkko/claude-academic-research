# Philseok Lee — George Mason University, USA

Philseok Lee is a quantitative I-O psychometrician at George Mason University (previously South Dakota State University; PhD-era work at the University of South Florida with Stephen Stark). The program has two strands: item response theory for non-cognitive measurement — especially multidimensional forced-choice (MFC) personality measures, ideal point (GGUM/GGUM-RANK) and Thurstonian IRT models, DIF, faking, and careless/aberrant responding — and, more recently, natural language processing and large language models applied to scale development, item generation, and content validation. A steady ORM contributor (6 articles).

**Representative method papers:**
- Lee, Joo & Stark (2020), "Detecting DIF in Multidimensional Forced Choice Measures Using the Thurstonian Item Response Theory Model," *Organizational Research Methods* — DIF detection for MFC measures.
- Lee, Joo, Stark & Chernyshenko (2018), "GGUM-RANK Statement and Person Parameter Estimation With Multidimensional Forced Choice Triplets," *Applied Psychological Measurement* — estimation for ideal-point MFC triplets.
- Joo, Lee, Park & Stark (2021), "Assessing Dimensionality of the Ideal Point Item Response Theory Model Using Posterior Predictive Model Checking," *Organizational Research Methods* — Bayesian model checking for ideal-point IRT.
- Fyffe, Lee & Kaplan (2023), "'Transforming' Personality Scale Development: Illustrating the Potential of State-of-the-Art Natural Language Processing," *Organizational Research Methods* — transformer models for scale development.
- Lee, Son, Zhou, Joo, Jia & Cheng (2025), "The Journey of Forced Choice Measurement Over 80 Years: Past, Present, and Future," *Organizational Research Methods* — review of forced-choice measurement.
- Lee, Joo & Son (2023), "Detecting Careless Respondents in Multidimensional Forced Choice Data: An Application of lz Person-Fit Statistic to the TIRT Model," *Journal of Business and Psychology* — person-fit for MFC data.
- Lee, Son & Jia (2025), "AI-powered Automatic Item Generation for Psychological Tests: A Conceptual Framework for an LLM-based Multi-Agent AIG System," *Journal of Business and Psychology* — LLM-based item generation.
- Son & Lee (2026), "Can Generative Large Language Models Serve as Raters for Test Development? A Systematic Evaluation Across Tasks, Models, and Inference Configurations," *Organizational Research Methods* — LLMs as content-validation raters.
