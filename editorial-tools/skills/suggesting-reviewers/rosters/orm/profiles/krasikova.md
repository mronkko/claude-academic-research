# Dina V. Krasikova — University of Texas at San Antonio, USA

Krasikova is a quantitative organizational-behavior scholar at UTSA whose work pairs substantive leadership questions (destructive leadership, narcissism, psychological capital, well-being) with attention to analytic alignment. Her most methodologically influential contributions concern the correct modeling of dyadic and interactive phenomena — flagging mismatches between level of theory and level of method, and extending relative importance analysis to higher-order effects. She publishes in *Journal of Management*, *Journal of Applied Psychology*, *OBHDP*, *Journal of Organizational Behavior*, and *ORM*, and serves on the ORM editorial team as Associate Editor. Her orientation is clearly quantitative (multilevel, mediation/indirect-effect, and dyadic designs), with a mid-career profile (first publication 2011, h-index 12, ~43 works, continuously at UTSA since 2015).

**Representative method papers:**
- LeBreton, Tonidandel & Krasikova (2013), "Residualized Relative Importance Analysis," *Organizational Research Methods* — extends relative importance statistics to models with interactive and higher-order (quadratic, cubic) effects.
- Krasikova & LeBreton (2012), "Just the two of us: Misalignment of theory and methods in examining dyadic phenomena," *Journal of Applied Psychology* — diagnoses theory/method misalignment in dyadic research and prescribes aligned analytic approaches.
- Harms, Krasikova & Luthans (2018), "Not Me, But Reflects Me: Validating a Simple Implicit Measure of Psychological Capital," *Journal of Personality Assessment* — measurement-validation paper for an implicit PsyCap instrument.

**Would referee well:** multilevel/HLM designs, mediation and indirect-effect modeling, dyadic data analysis, relative importance and variance-decomposition methods, measurement validation, and leadership/PsyCap substantive work using these techniques.
