# Ann Langley — HEC Montréal, Canada

Ann Langley is one of the most influential qualitative and process methodologists in organization studies. Her 1999 *Academy of Management Review* article on strategies for theorizing from process data (~7,000 citations) is the canonical reference for analyzing longitudinal, event-based qualitative data, and her later work extends this into process ontology, temporality, and the craft of building theory from qualitative material. Substantively she studies strategic change, collective/plural leadership, strategy as practice, and health-care organizations. Long-time professor at HEC Montréal (earlier at UQAM), with recurring visiting/affiliated ties to the University of Warwick and the University of Sydney.

**Representative method papers:**
- Langley (1999), "Strategies for Theorizing from Process Data," *Academy of Management Review* — seven sensemaking strategies (narrative, visual mapping, temporal bracketing, etc.) for theorizing from process data; her flagship contribution.
- Langley, Smallman, Tsoukas & Van de Ven (2013), "Process Studies of Change in Organization and Management: Unveiling Temporality, Activity, and Flow," *Academy of Management Journal* — framing and agenda for process research designs.
- Gehman, Glaser, Eisenhardt, Gioia, Langley & Corley (2017), "Finding Theory–Method Fit: A Comparison of Three Qualitative Approaches to Theory Building," *Journal of Management Inquiry* — contrasts the Eisenhardt, Gioia, and Langley approaches to qualitative theory building.
- Langley & Abdallah (2011), "Templates and Turns in Qualitative Studies of Strategy and Management," *Research Methodology in Strategy and Management* — dominant templates (positivist case, Gioia) and interpretive "turns" in qualitative writing.
- Klag & Langley (2012), "Approaching the Conceptual Leap in Qualitative Research," *International Journal of Management Reviews* — how qualitative researchers move from data to abstraction.
- Langley & Klag (2017), "Being Where? Navigating the Involvement Paradox in Qualitative Research Accounts," *Organizational Research Methods* — how researchers portray their own involvement in qualitative accounts.
- Cloutier, Langley & Corley (2026), *How to Design, Write and Publish Qualitative Research for Insight and Impact* (Edward Elgar) — book-length guide to designing, writing, and publishing theory-driven qualitative research.
