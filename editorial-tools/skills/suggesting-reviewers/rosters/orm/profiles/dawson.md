# Jeremy F. Dawson — University of Sheffield, UK

Jeremy Dawson is a senior quantitative methodologist (h-index 46, ~15,200 citations across 239 works) whose work bridges applied organizational psychology and applied statistics. He is best known for practical guidance on the use and interpretation of interaction effects in regression — most notably the development of the slope-difference test for three-way interactions and a widely cited primer on moderation in management research. Alongside this methods stream he runs a substantive program on organizational climate, team performance, workplace diversity, and the link between HR/management practices and outcomes (including health-care/NHS settings). His later work extends into multilevel and longitudinal modeling and risk-of-bias appraisal of statistical/ML prediction models. He sits as an Associate Editor at ORM.

**Representative method papers:**
- Dawson & Richter (2006), "Probing three-way interactions in moderated multiple regression: Development and application of a slope difference test," _Journal of Applied Psychology_ — derives a significance test for slope differences in three-way interactions and validates power via Monte Carlo simulation (his signature methods contribution).
- Dawson (2014), "Moderation in management research: What, why, when, and how," _Journal of Business and Psychology_ — a heavily cited applied tutorial on specifying, testing, probing, and plotting moderation/interaction effects.
- Patterson, West, Shackleton, Dawson, et al. (2005), "Validating the organizational climate measure," _Journal of Organizational Behavior_ — development and psychometric validation of a multidimensional climate scale (n≈6,869), a measurement-development contribution.
- Lawrence, Bacila, Tonge, Tucker, Dawson (2025), "Risk of bias in machine learning and statistical models to predict height or weight: a systematic review," _Diagnostic and Prognostic Research_ — systematic appraisal of prediction-model methodology / risk of bias.
