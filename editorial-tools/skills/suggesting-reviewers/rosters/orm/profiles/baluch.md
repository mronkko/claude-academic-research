# Alina Baluch — University of St. Andrews, UK

Qualitative organization and HRM researcher (University of St Andrews since 2012; earlier Leibniz University Hannover). Substantive work centers on strategic human resource management in nonprofit and hybrid organizations, employee perceptions of HRM, and organizational identity in nonprofit–business partnerships. Methodological contributions, mostly with Christina Hoon and Hans-Gerd Ridder, concern how qualitative case studies build theory, how review studies can theorize rather than summarize, and qualitative research designs that span several studies. Some earlier HRM–performance work is quantitative (e.g., a 2013 *J. of Applied Psychology* paper), but the methods profile is qualitative.

**Representative method papers:**
- Hoon & Baluch (2023), "Macro-iterativity: A Qualitative Multi-arc Design for Studying Complex Issues and Big Questions," ORM — a qualitative design that links several studies (arcs) to tackle big questions.
- Hoon & Baluch (2019), "The Role of Dialectical Interrogation in Review Studies: Theorizing from What We See Rather Than What We Have Already Seen," J. of Management Studies — review methodology aimed at building theory.
- Ridder, Hoon & Baluch (2012), "Entering a Dialogue: Positioning Case Study Findings towards Theory," British J. of Management — how case-study findings contribute to theory.
- Ridder, Hoon & Baluch (2009), "The theoretical contribution of case study research to the field of strategy and management," Research Methodology in Strategy and Management (book chapter) — the theoretical contribution of case studies.
- Baluch & Ridder (2020), "Mapping the Research Landscape of Strategic Human Resource Management in Nonprofit Organizations: A Systematic Review and Avenues for Future Research," Nonprofit and Voluntary Sector Quarterly — an applied systematic review.

**Would referee well:** qualitative case-study and theory-building designs, multi-study qualitative research designs, literature-review and integrative-review methods, qualitative studies of HRM, identity, memory work and nonprofit/hybrid organizations.
