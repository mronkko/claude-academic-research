# Justin A. DeSimone — University of Alabama System, USA

Justin DeSimone is a quantitative organizational-research methodologist at the University of Alabama whose signature contributions concern survey data quality — detecting and managing careless / insufficient-effort responding (IER), best-practice data screening, and the appropriate consumption and reporting of meta-analytic evidence. His most-cited work, "Best practice recommendations for data screening" (JOB, 2014), and follow-ups on response speed/consistency and the differential impacts of IER make him one of the field's go-to authorities on cleaning and trusting survey data. He also works on measurement (conditional-reasoning / implicit-trait assessment, resilience-scale validation) and on how organizational scholars should review and cite meta-analyses. As a sitting ORM Associate Editor he is reviewer-ineligible; this profile feeds AE-mode matching.

**Representative method papers:**
- DeSimone, Harms & DeSimone (2014), "Best practice recommendations for data screening," *Journal of Organizational Behavior* — taxonomy of survey data-screening techniques; the field's reference for handling low-quality respondents.
- DeSimone & Harms (2017), "Dirty Data: The Effects of Screening Respondents Who Provide Low-Quality Data," *Journal of Business and Psychology* — quantifies the consequences of (not) screening careless responders.
- Wood, Harms, Lowman & DeSimone (2017), "Response Speed and Response Consistency as Mutually Validating Indicators of Data Quality," *Social Psychological and Personality Science* — operationalizes careless responding via speed/consistency in online samples.
- DeSimone, DeSimone, Harms & Wood (2017), "The Differential Impacts of Two Forms of Insufficient Effort Responding," *Applied Psychology* — simulation contrasting straightlining vs. random responding.
- DeSimone, Brannick, O'Boyle & Ryu (2020), "Recommendations for Reviewing Meta-Analyses in Organizational Research," *Organizational Research Methods* — transparency/reproducibility checklist for meta-analytic reporting.
- DeSimone, Köhler & Schoen (2018), "If It Were Only That Easy: The Use of Meta-Analytic Research by Organizational Scholars," *Organizational Research Methods* — critique of how MA results are (mis)interpreted and cited.
