# Thomas Greckhamer — Louisiana State University, USA

Greckhamer is a leading methodologist of qualitative comparative analysis (QCA) and the configurational / set-theoretic approach in management and organization research, with a secondary base in interpretive and discourse-analytic qualitative methods. His most-cited work consists of best-practice and theory-building guides for fsQCA in strategy and organization research (1,200+ and 700+ citations), and he applies these methods substantively to cross-national compensation, CEO pay, and stakeholder/institutional configurations. He is a current ORM Associate Editor (eligible: no — excluded from reviewer suggestions).

**Representative method papers:**
- Greckhamer, Furnari, Fiss & Aguilera (2018), "Studying configurations with qualitative comparative analysis: Best practices in strategy and organization research," *Strategic Organization* — canonical best-practice guide for the full QCA workflow (model building, calibration, analysis, reporting).
- Misangyi, Greckhamer, Furnari, Fiss, Crilly et al. (2016), "Embracing Causal Complexity," *Journal of Management* — articulates the neo-configurational perspective and causal-complexity rationale for QCA.
- Furnari, Crilly, Misangyi, Greckhamer, Fiss (2020), "Capturing Causal Complexity: Heuristics for Configurational Theorizing," *Academy of Management Review* — heuristics for building configurational theory.
- Greckhamer, Misangyi & Fiss (2013), "The Two QCAs: From a Small-N to a Large-N Set Theoretic Approach," *Research in the Sociology of Organizations* — bridges small-N and large-N QCA for organization studies.
- Greckhamer, Misangyi, Elms & Lacey (2007), "Using Qualitative Comparative Analysis in Strategic Management Research," *Organizational Research Methods* — early demonstration of QCA on a large-N strategy dataset.

**Would referee well:** QCA / fsQCA, configurational and set-theoretic methods, causal complexity and equifinality, calibration and robustness in set-theoretic analysis, configurational theorizing; also qualitative methodology, discourse/interpretive methods, and methodological socialization in doctoral training.

**Notes:** Orientation is methodologist with a qualitative/configurational center of gravity rather than quantitative-statistical — best matched to set-theoretic, configurational, or qualitative methods papers, not to psychometric/SEM/multilevel-modeling submissions.
