# Jeffrey M. Stanton — Syracuse University, USA

Jeffrey M. Stanton is a quantitative methodologist and organizational data scientist in the iSchool at Syracuse University. His method work spans survey research (internet data collection, nonresponse-bias assessment), psychometric scale construction and reduction, time-series/within-person designs, equivalence and null-confirming inference, and—more recently—data science with Python and the use of AI/LLMs in measurement-scale development. Substantively he is best known for employee performance monitoring, privacy/security behavior, and knowledge management, but the recurring thread is rigorous measurement and quantitative analysis. With works_count 161, an h-index of 42, and a first publication in 1998, he is a senior scholar. As a sitting ORM Associate Editor he is **not** eligible to be suggested as a reviewer; he is an AE-pool candidate only.

**Representative method papers:**
- Stanton, Sinar, Balzer & Smith (2002), "Issues and Strategies for Reducing the Length of Self-Report Scales," _Personnel Psychology_ — foundational guidance on principled scale shortening while preserving psychometric quality.
- Stanton (1998), "An Empirical Assessment of Data Collection Using the Internet," _Personnel Psychology_ — early validation of web-based survey data collection against paper administration.
- Rogelberg & Stanton (2007), "Introduction [Understanding and Dealing with Organizational Survey Nonresponse]," _Organizational Research Methods_ — the N-BIAS framework for assessing nonresponse-bias impact and external validity.
- Stanton (2020), "Evaluating Equivalence and Confirming the Null in the Organizational Sciences," _Organizational Research Methods_ — frequentist and Bayesian techniques for equivalence/null-confirming tests and the power planning they imply.
- Stanton (2026), "Mini-review: Considering Impacts of Artificial Intelligence on the Development of Measurement Scales," _Frontiers in Organizational Psychology_ — promise and pitfalls of LLMs for item generation, validity assessment, and adaptive testing.
