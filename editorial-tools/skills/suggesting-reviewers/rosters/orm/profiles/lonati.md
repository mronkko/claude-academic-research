# Sirio Lonati — NEOMA Business School, France

Sirio Lonati is an early-career quantitative methodologist at NEOMA Business School (PhD University of Lausanne, 2020, in John Antonakis's causal-inference group). The methods work centers on causal identification in management research — rigorous experimental design, instrumental variables and treatment-effect heterogeneity, sensitivity analysis for omitted confounders (the impact threshold of a confounding variable, ITCV), and the distributional assumptions of latent interaction models. A substantive strand studies the cultural and ecological origins of leadership styles.

**Representative method papers:**
- Lonati, Quiroga, Zehnder & Antonakis (2018), "On Doing Relevant and Rigorous Experiments: Review and Recommendations," *Journal of Operations Management* — design and analysis guidelines for experiments; most-cited work.
- Wulff, Sajons, Pogrebna, Lonati, Bastardoz, Banks & Antonakis (2023), "Common Methodological Mistakes," *The Leadership Quarterly* — catalogue of frequent design and inference errors with remedies.
- Lonati, Rönkkö & Antonakis (2024), "Normality Assumption in Latent Interaction Models," *Psychological Methods* — consequences of non-normality for latent moderated SEM estimators.
- Lonati & Wulff (2024), "Hic Sunt Dracones: On the Risks of Comparing the ITCV With Control Variable Correlations," *Journal of Management* — critique of a common ITCV sensitivity-analysis practice.
- Lonati, Lalive & Efferson (2024), "Identifying Culture as Cause: Challenges and Opportunities," *Evolutionary Human Sciences* — causal identification strategies for cultural effects.

**Would referee well:** causal inference and endogeneity; lab/field experiments; instrumental variables and LATE; omitted-variable sensitivity analysis (ITCV, robustness to confounding); latent interaction / moderated SEM; methodological-mistakes and reporting critiques; cross-cultural and leadership research using quasi-experimental or historical data.
