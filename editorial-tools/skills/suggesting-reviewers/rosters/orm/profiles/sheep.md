# Matthew Sheep — Florida Gulf Coast University, USA

Qualitative organizational scholar working on individual and organizational identity, identity work and boundary dynamics, paradox and organizational tensions, discursive/communicative approaches to organizing (including CCO theory and leadership communication), and workplace spirituality. Publishes as "Mathew Laurence Sheep" (single "t"); the board page spells the name "Matthew". Career runs from the University of Cincinnati (doctorate, 2001–2006) through Illinois State University to Florida Gulf Coast University (2017–present). Best known for the Kreiner, Hollensbe & Sheep qualitative identity studies in *AMJ* (the 2009 boundary-work article has 1,100+ citations). This is a substantive scholar who uses qualitative methods, not a methodologist who develops them; there are no stand-alone methods papers in the record.

**Representative method papers** (closest methodological contributions in the record):
- Corlett, McInnes, Coupland & Sheep (2017), "Exploring the Registers of Identity Research," *International Journal of Management Reviews*: a review that maps the ontological and epistemological registers identity research is conducted in.
- Sheep, Fairhurst & Khazanchi (2016), "Knots in the Discourse of Innovation: Investigating Multiple Tensions in a Reacquired Spin-off," *Organization Studies*: a discourse-analytic approach to tracing interwoven ("knotted") paradoxical tensions.
- Sheep, Rheinhardt, Hollensbe & Kreiner (2022), "'Tearing the Fabric' or 'Weaving the Tapestry'? A Discursive Resources Approach to Identity-Implicating Organizational Events," *Management Communication Quarterly*: a discursive-resources analytic lens on identity talk.
- Kreiner, Hollensbe & Sheep (2009), "Balancing Borders and Bridges: Negotiating the Work-Home Interface via Boundary Work Tactics," *Academy of Management Journal*: an exemplar inductive, grounded-theory interview study (with Kreiner et al. 2006, *AMJ*).
- Sheep (2004), "Nailing Down Gossamer: A Valid Measure of the Person-Organization Fit of Workplace Spirituality," *AoM Proceedings*: scale development for P-O fit of workplace spirituality, the only quantitative measurement contribution in the record.

**Would referee well:** qualitative identity and identity-work studies, inductive/grounded-theory interview designs, discourse and discursive-psychology analysis, paradox/tension research, communication-as-constitutive (CCO) approaches, qualitative work on spirituality or religion in organizations.
