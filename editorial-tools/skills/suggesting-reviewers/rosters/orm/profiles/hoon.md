# Christina Hoon — University of Bielefeld, Germany

Christina Hoon is a qualitative management scholar who holds a chair in family business at Bielefeld. Her substantive work covers family firms (stewardship, succession, identity, narrative memory), strategy-as-practice, and HRM; her methodological reputation rests on qualitative case-study research — above all her ORM meta-synthesis procedure for building theory from published qualitative case studies, which is the standard citation for that approach. Later method work addresses positioning case findings toward theory, dialectical interrogation in review studies, and multi-arc qualitative designs for big questions.

**Representative method papers:**
- Hoon (2013), "Meta-Synthesis of Qualitative Case Studies," *Organizational Research Methods* — eight-step procedure for synthesizing qualitative case studies into theory; her flagship (400+ citations).
- Ridder, Hoon & McCandless Baluch (2012), "Entering a Dialogue: Positioning Case Study Findings towards Theory," *British Journal of Management* — how case studies contribute to theory.
- Ridder, Hoon & McCandless Baluch (2009), "The theoretical contribution of case study research to the field of strategy and management," *Research Methodology in Strategy and Management* — case-study theorizing in strategy.
- Hoon & Baluch (2019), "The Role of Dialectical Interrogation in Review Studies: Theorizing from What We See Rather Than What We Have Already Seen," *Journal of Management Studies* — review methodology for theorizing.
- Hoon & Baluch (2023), "Macro-iterativity: A Qualitative Multi-arc Design for Studying Complex Issues and Big Questions," *Organizational Research Methods* — multi-study iterative qualitative design.

**Would referee well:** qualitative case-study design and theorizing; qualitative meta-synthesis and meta-studies; literature-review and integrative-review methodology; multi-study qualitative designs; qualitative family-business, strategy-process, and identity research.

**Notes:** Formerly Leibniz University Hannover (with Hans-Gerd Ridder). Frequent co-author Alina McCandless Baluch. Publishes partly in German (books on family-business management). Qualitative orientation; not a fit for statistical or psychometric manuscripts, though apt for quantitative-vs-qualitative synthesis questions at the review level.
