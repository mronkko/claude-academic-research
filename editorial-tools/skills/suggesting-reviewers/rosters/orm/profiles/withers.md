# Michael C. Withers — Texas A&M University, USA

Strategy and corporate-governance scholar (boards of directors, director selection, executives, ownership, family firms, resource dependence) who doubles as a methodologist for management research. His most-cited work spans both a substantive governance review program (resource dependence theory, board interlocks, director selection) and a high-impact methods program on causal-inference pitfalls in observational strategy data — endogeneity and instrumental variables, omitted-variable bias, within- vs. between-firm effects in panels, exogenous shocks, and Monte Carlo evaluation of methods. The board listing places him at Texas A&M (his affiliation 2011–2025, with the Mitchell Institute); OpenAlex shows a recent move to the University of Notre Dame (2024–2026). Senior (h-index 28, 7,000+ citations, ~78 works since 2009). Tagged `eligible: no` — sitting Associate Editor, never suggested as a reviewer; available in AE mode only.

**Representative method papers:**
- Semadeni, Withers & Certo (2013), "The perils of endogeneity and instrumental variables in strategy research: Understanding through simulations," _Strategic Management Journal_ — simulation-based demonstration of endogeneity bias and the weak-instrument power tradeoff.
- Busenbark, Yoon, Gamache & Withers (2021), "Omitted Variable Bias: Examining Management Research With the Impact Threshold of a Confounding Variable (ITCV)," _Journal of Management_ — argues for ITCV as an alternative to two-stage IV for diagnosing omitted-variable endogeneity.
- Certo, Withers & Semadeni (2016), "A tale of two effects: Using longitudinal data to compare within- and between-firm effects," _Strategic Management Journal_ — introduces the hybrid (within/between) approach for panel strategy data.
- Jacquart, Santoni, Schudy, Sieweke & Withers (2024), "Exogenous shocks: Definitions, types, and causal identification issues," _The Leadership Quarterly_ — typology of exogenous shocks and their causal-identification requirements.
- Certo, Busenbark, Li & Withers (2026), "A primer on using Monte Carlo simulations to evaluate research methods in strategic management," _Strategic Management Review_ — tutorial on Monte Carlo evaluation of methods.

**Would referee well:** endogeneity and instrumental-variable design, omitted-variable bias / sensitivity analysis (ITCV), panel and longitudinal modeling (within/between, fixed effects), event/quasi-experimental and exogenous-shock identification, and the broad substantive governance literature (boards, director selection, ownership, family firms, resource dependence).
